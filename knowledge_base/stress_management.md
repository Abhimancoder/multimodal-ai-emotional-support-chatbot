# Evidence-Based Stress Management and Coping Strategies

## 1. Cognitive Reframing (Perspective Shifting)
Cognitive reframing is a cognitive-behavioral practice that helps identify automatic cognitive distortions (such as catastrophizing or all-or-nothing thinking) and re-evaluates them with balanced perspectives.

### How to Practice:
1. **Notice the Thought:** Write down the negative automatic thought (e.g., "I am failing everything and this exam will ruin my future").
2. **Examine the Evidence:** Ask yourself: "What objective facts support this thought? What objective facts contradict it?"
3. **Generate a Balanced Thought:** Replace extreme assumptions with realistic, compassionate framing (e.g., "This exam is difficult, but one exam does not define my worth or future. I can study in 25-minute intervals and do my best").

---

## 2. The "Worry Time" Technique
When worries intrude continuously throughout study or daily work:
1. Designate a specific 15-minute window each day (e.g., 4:30 PM to 4:45 PM) as your official "Worry Time".
2. When an anxious thought surfaces during the day, write it down in a notebook and tell yourself: "I will consider this during my worry time, not right now."
3. During the designated 15 minutes, review your list. Many items will have lost their urgency; for remaining genuine concerns, brainstorm one actionable step.
4. Stop when the timer sounds.

---

## 3. Emotional Journaling & Brain Dumping
Putting unstructured thoughts onto physical paper reduces working-memory cognitive load:
- Spend 5 to 10 minutes writing without filtering, editing, or judging grammar.
- Acknowledge feelings without criticizing yourself for having them.
- Close the notebook as a physical symbol of having safely released the mental clutter.
