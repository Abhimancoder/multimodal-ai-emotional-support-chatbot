# Healthy Communication and Interpersonal Boundaries

## 1. Using "I" Statements
When expressing frustration or distress to others, "You" statements often provoke defensiveness (e.g., "You never listen to me").
"I" statements clearly convey your emotions and needs without casting blame:

### The 4-Part Structure:
1. **"I feel..."** (Name the emotion without judgment: hurt, overwhelmed, left out).
2. **"When..."** (Describe the specific observable situation, not character flaws).
3. **"Because..."** (Explain the practical or emotional effect on you).
4. **"What I need is..."** (State a constructive, actionable request).

*Example:* "I feel stressed when group work tasks are left until the night before the deadline because I worry we will miss it. What I need is for us to agree on milestones two days in advance."

---

## 2. Active and Reflective Listening
Building healthy connection requires listening to understand rather than simply waiting for your turn to speak:
- **Reflect Back:** Paraphrase what you heard before replying (e.g., "It sounds like you felt really dismissed during that meeting").
- **Validate:** Acknowledge that their reaction makes sense given their experience.
- **Ask Clarifying Questions:** "Would you like me to just listen, or would you like to brainstorm options together?"

---

## 3. Setting Compassionate Boundaries
Boundaries protect your emotional energy and well-being:
- Clearly communicate what you have the capacity for (e.g., "I care about you, but I cannot take calls after 10 PM because I need rest").
- You do not need to over-explain or apologize for having personal physical or emotional limits.
